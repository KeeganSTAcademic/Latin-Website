The future tense

The Latin future tense describes incomplete action happening at some point later in time. We translate it either like "he will love" (simple future) or "he will be loving" (ongoing future).

It is formed in the 1st (-āre) and 2nd (-ēre) conjugation groups by adding a -bi- time marker between the incomplete stem and the same personal endings. Note that the -i- in -bi- is the same time marker as in the English "will," which can be a useful mnemonic for remembering the future's translation in English.

Note that the 1st singular form is -bō and the 3rd plural -bunt. Linguists think that in the former case the ō absorbed the expected -i-, and in the latter that the Romans changed the -i- to a -u-; a similar shift can be seen in the 3rd plural of the 3rd conjugation's present (regunt).

The 3rd and 4th conjugations build their future differently (regam, regēs; audiam, audiēs). They will get their own page.

Irregular: sum, esse (to be)

sum has no -bi-, but its future shows the same -i- time marker on the stem er-, with the same -ō and -unt: erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis, erunt.
