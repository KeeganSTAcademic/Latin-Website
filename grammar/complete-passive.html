The complete passives are the passive forms of the perfect, the pluperfect and the future perfect. Unlike the incomplete passives, they are not single words: each one is a participle with a form of sum, as in amātus est, "he has been loved." Like the perfect active, the perfect passive also has a simple-past translation: amātus est can mean "he was loved."

The forms

How to build it

Take the 4th principal part, the perfect passive participle (amātus, -a, -um, "loved"), and add a form of sum. The participle carries the complete aspect, and sum carries the time, person and number: the present sum for the perfect, the imperfect eram for the pluperfect, and the future erō for the future perfect. Because the participle is an adjective, its ending agrees with the subject: amātus est for a man, amāta est for a woman, amātī sunt for a group.

The basic use: translating the complete passive A&G §473

Translate the perfect passive as "has been loved" or "was loved," the pluperfect passive as "had been loved," and the future perfect passive as "will have been loved." Do not translate the form of sum on its own: amātus est does not mean "he is loved."

How to spot it: Look for a participle ending in -tus or -sus (or -ta, -tum, -tī, -tae) next to a form of sum. The two words can be separated, and the form of sum sometimes comes first.

Puella laudāta est.
The girl has been praised, or the girl was praised.
Urbs iam dēlēta erat.
The city had already been destroyed.
Crās epistulae missae erunt.
Tomorrow the letters will have been sent.

Other uses

The participle agrees with the subject

A&G §286

The participle is an adjective, so it agrees with the subject in gender, number and case. It is always nominative here, but its gender and number change with the subject.

Clue: Match the participle's ending to the subject: -us or -ī for masculine, -a or -ae for feminine, -um or -a for neuter.

Mīlitēs vocātī sunt.
The soldiers were called.
Oppida capta sunt.
The towns were captured.

The person doing the action

A&G §405

As with the incomplete passives, Latin uses ā or ab with the ablative to say who performed the action. English uses "by."

Clue: Look for ā or ab followed by a person in the ablative, near the participle.

Urbs ā Rōmānīs capta est.
The city was captured by the Romans.
