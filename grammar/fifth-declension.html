Fifth Declension

The smallest declension, built on the vowel ē, which turns up in every case: rēs, reī, reī, rem, rē. It has only a handful of nouns, nearly all feminine, but two of them are among the commonest words in Latin: rēs (thing, matter) and diēs (day). The genitive and dative singular are -eī after a consonant (reī) and -ēī after a vowel (diēī).

diēs and the long ē
  • -eī or -ēī? The stem's ē stays long in the genitive and dative singular when a vowel comes before it (diēī), but shortens after a consonant in rēs, fidēs, spēs (reī, fideī, speī). In the accusative singular it is always short: rem, diem (A&G §96).
  • Gender. diēs is usually masculine, but it can be feminine for a fixed day (cōnstitūtā diē, "on the appointed day") and regularly is when it means time in general (longa diēs, "a long time") (A&G §97).
  • Missing plurals. Only rēs and diēs are declined in full. Most other 5th declension nouns have no plural, though a few (aciēs, speciēs, spēs) have a nominative and accusative plural (A&G §98).
