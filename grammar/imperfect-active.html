was loving, used to loveA&G §§470–471

The imperfect describes something that was going on, or used to happen, in the past: amābat, "she was loving" or "she used to love." In a story it usually describes the background, while the perfect reports the main events.

The forms

Irregular: sum, esse (to be)

sum doesn't use -ba-. Its imperfect is built on er-, with the same endings as every other imperfect: eram, erās, erat, erāmus, erātis, erant. You'll see erat constantly in stories: puella in hortō erat, "the girl was in the garden."

How to build it

The imperfect uses the same stem and personal endings as the present, with -ba- placed between them. The one different ending is -m, not -ō, for "I." The a in -ba- is the time vowel: Latin tends to use a for past time (compare eram, "I was"), and the same vowel appears in English "was," which can help you remember it.

The endings are -m (I), -s (you), -t (he/she/it), -mus (we), -tis (you all) and -nt (they).

In the 3rd conjugation the linking vowel goes back to -ē- (regēbam), and the 3rd -iō and 4th conjugations use -iē- (capiēbam, audiēbam).

The basic use: choosing the English translation A&G §470

Use "was ___ing" for an action in progress at a moment in the past, "used to ___" for a habit, and "kept ___ing" for an action repeated again and again. If none of these sounds natural, the simple past ("she loved") is often fine, as long as the action is not a single, finished event.

How to spot it: Look for -ba- between the stem and the personal ending.

Puella cantābat.
The girl was singing.
Cotīdiē ambulābāmus.
We used to walk every day.
Nox erat; mīlitēs dormiēbant.
It was night; the soldiers were sleeping.

Other uses

An action continuing up to a past moment

A&G §471

With iam diū or iam prīdem, the imperfect describes an action that had already been going on for some time at a moment in the past. English uses "had been ___ing" for this.

Clue: Look for iam diū, iam prīdem or a length of time next to an imperfect verb.

Iam diū tē exspectābam.
I had been waiting for you for a long time.

An attempted or beginning action

A&G §471

Because the imperfect shows an action that was not finished, it can describe an attempt ("tried to ___") or an action that was just beginning ("began to ___").

Clue: The context shows that the action did not succeed or had only just started.

Hostēs urbem capiēbant.
The enemy were trying to take the city.
