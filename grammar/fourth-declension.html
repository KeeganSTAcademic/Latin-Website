Fourth Declension

An interactive grammar for frūctus and cornū

A small declension built on u, which shows up in almost every ending. Most nouns are masculine in -us; manus (hand) and domus (house) are common feminine exceptions.

Exceptions worth knowing
  • -ubus for -ibus. The stem u usually weakens to i before -bus (cornibus), but lacus lake regularly keeps lacubus, as do artus (and partus, tribus); portus and specus have both (A&G §§88, 92.c).
  • Feminine nouns in -us. Most are masculine, but manus hand, domus house, porticus colonnade, tribus tribe, Īdūs the Ides, and a few others are feminine (A&G §90). Adjectives agreeing with them are feminine: manus dextra.
  • Only four neuters. cornū horn, genū knee, pecū flock, verū spit (A&G §91). If a 4th declension noun ends in -ū, it's neuter.
  • domus mixes declensions. It has both 4th and 2nd declension forms (A&G §93).
  • Shortened genitive plural. In poetry -uum is sometimes squeezed into one syllable and written -um: currum for curruum (A&G §92.b).
  • 2nd declension look-alikes. Some nouns in -tus occasionally have a 2nd declension genitive: senātī for senātūs (A&G §92.a).
