Future Active Indicative

View
will love, will be lovingA&G §§472

The future describes something that will happen later: amābit, "he will love" or "he will be loving." The 1st and 2nd conjugations mark the future with -bi-; the 3rd and 4th conjugations mark it with a vowel instead (regam, regēs; audiam, audiēs).

The forms

Irregular: sum, esse (to be)

sum has no -bi-, but its future shows the same -i- time marker on the stem er-, with the same -ō and -unt: erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis, erunt.

How to build it

In the 1st and 2nd conjugations, the future places -bi- between the stem and the personal ending. The i in -bi- is the time vowel: Latin tends to use i for future time (compare erit, "he will be"), and the same vowel appears in English "will," which can help you remember it.

Two forms change the -i-: the 1st singular ends in -bō and the 3rd plural in -bunt. In amābō the ō seems to have swallowed the i; in amābunt it became u, the same change as regunt in the present.

The 3rd, 3rd -iō and 4th conjugations do not use -bi-. Their future is marked by a vowel shift: -a- in the 1st singular and -ē- in the other forms, shortened to -e- before -t and -nt: regam, regēs, reget, regēmus, regētis, regent. These vowels are not the time vowels described above; the a of regam does not mean past. Watch the look-alikes this creates: reget, "he will rule," and regit, "he rules," differ by one vowel.

The basic use: choosing the English translation A&G §472

"Will ___" is usually the natural translation. Use "will be ___ing" when the sentence stresses that the action will be in progress at some time in the future.

How to spot it: In the 1st and 2nd conjugations, look for -bi- between the stem and the personal ending, with -bō for "I" and -bunt for "they." In the 3rd and 4th conjugations, look for -am for "I" and an ē (or e) before the other endings.

Crās labōrābimus.
We will work tomorrow.
Tē semper amābō.
I will always love you.

Other uses

A polite command

A&G §449

As in English, a sentence such as "you will take care of this" can give an order or an instruction.

Clue: A 2nd-person future verb is used to tell someone what to do.

Tū hoc cūrābis.
You will take care of this.

In "if" and "when" clauses

A&G §472

Latin is more exact than English here. When the action of an "if" or "when" clause is still in the future, Latin puts that verb in the future tense, while English uses the present.

Clue: Look for a future verb after sī, cum or ubi.

Sī mē vocābis, aderō.
If you call me, I will be there.
