Pluperfect Subjunctive

Highlight a pattern
View
amāvisset: had loved, would have lovedA&G §§441, 483, 517, 546

The pluperfect subjunctive is the complete subjunctive of secondary sequence. In a dependent clause after a past main verb, it describes an action earlier than the main verb: rogāvī quid amāvisset, "I asked what he had loved." It also expresses things that did not happen: a wish about the past and the past contrary-to-fact condition.

For what the subjunctive is and how sequence of tenses works, see The Subjunctive: Overview. Every use is listed on Uses of the Subjunctive.

The forms

Irregular: sum, esse (to be)

sum is regular here: the perfect infinitive fuisse plus the endings, fuissem, fuissēs, fuisset.

How to build it

Take the perfect active infinitive (amāvisse: the complete stem plus -isse) and add the personal endings: amāvisse-t. The passive is the perfect participle with the imperfect subjunctive of sum: amātus essem, amātus esset.

The basic use: after a past main verb A&G §483

In a dependent clause after a past main verb, the pluperfect subjunctive shows an action earlier than the main verb. Translate it with "had ___ed": rogāvī quid fēcisset, "I asked what he had done."

How to spot it: Look for -isse- between the complete stem and the ending (amāvisset, rēxisset), or a participle with essem, esset.

Rogāvī quid fēcissēs.
I asked what you had done.
Cum id nūntiātum esset, mātūrat.
When this had been reported, he hurries. Caesar, B.G. 1.7

Other uses

Wishes about the past

A&G §441

The pluperfect subjunctive wishes that the past had been different, and implies that it was not. Utinam regularly comes first. English uses "if only ... had."

Clue: Utinam with a pluperfect subjunctive.

Utinam adfuissēs!
If only you had been there!

Conditions that were not true

A&G §517

In "if" sentences about the past, the pluperfect subjunctive in both halves says that the condition was not true. English uses "if ... had" and "would have."

Clue: Sī with a pluperfect subjunctive in both halves of the sentence.

Sī vēnissēs, tē vīdissem.
If you had come, I would have seen you.

Other forms you'll see
