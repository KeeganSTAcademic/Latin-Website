is loved, was being loved, will be lovedA&G §§163, 405, 409

In the passive voice, the subject receives the action instead of doing it: "the girl is loved," not "the girl loves." This page covers the passive of the present, the imperfect and the future.

The forms

How to build it

The passive uses the same stem, stem vowel and time marker as the active; only the personal ending is different. Amā-ba-t, "he was loving," becomes amā-bā-tur, "he was being loved." Most passive endings look like the active endings with an r added; only -minī is completely different. In the 3rd and 4th conjugations the future passive uses the same vowel shift as the future active in place of -bi-: regar, regēris, regētur.

The basic use: translating the passive A&G §163

Translate the present passive as "is loved" or "is being loved," the imperfect passive as "was being loved" or "used to be loved," and the future passive as "will be loved."

How to spot it: Look for an r in the personal ending (-r, -ris, -tur, -mur, -ntur), or for the ending -minī.

Puella amātur.
The girl is loved.
Urbs dēlēbātur.
The city was being destroyed.
Mīlitēs vocābuntur.
The soldiers will be called.

Other uses

The person doing the action

A&G §405

To say who performs the action of a passive verb, Latin uses ā or ab with the ablative. English uses "by."

Clue: Look for ā or ab followed by a person in the ablative, near a passive verb.

Puella ā mātre amātur.
The girl is loved by her mother.

The thing used to do the action

A&G §409

To say what thing or means is used, Latin uses the ablative on its own, without a preposition. English uses "by" or "with."

Clue: Look for a thing in the ablative, with no preposition, near a passive verb.

Urbs flammīs dēlēbātur.
The city was being destroyed by flames.
